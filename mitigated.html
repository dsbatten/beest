Bitcoin Network Emissions Mitigated

According to Cambridge, methane mitigation by Bitcoin mining reached 4.7 MtCO2e a year in December 2025 (using methane's 20-year warming potential and the latest peer-reviewed research on flare-gas efficiency).

In this chart I track how much of the network's total emissions is offset by the methane miners destroy at oil-well flares. At 100%, the network is carbon neutral; above 100%, it is carbon negative. I'll add landfill methane as a second line once a sourced figure exists.

  • How I calculate itEach day I divide the flare-gas mitigation by total emissions, with total emissions taken on a 30-day average of hashrate. I value methane at its 20-year warming potential, using the field-measured 91.1% flare destruction efficiency.
  • Why I use a 30-day hashrate average hereFlare-gas mining is off-grid and runs almost continuously. It doesn't switch off when grid power prices spike, so it doesn't follow the short swings in the network's hashrate. A 30-day average keeps those swings from moving the share when flare-gas mining itself hasn't changed. My other charts use the 7-day average.
  • Why the line is segmented, then jaggedUp to 26 October 2024, I take the share from BEEST 1, which estimated mitigation and emissions at set dates; I join those dates with straight lines, which is why that part moves in straight segments. From then on, I treat mitigation as a yearly tonnage (rising to Cambridge's 4.69 MtCO2e a year at December 2025, then held until the next survey), while total emissions move with the 30-day hashrate average. So the share rises when total emissions fall, and falls when they rise, even when flare-gas mining hasn't changed.

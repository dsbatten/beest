Bitcoin Mining: Usage of Sustainable Energy

In this chart I track the share of mining power that comes from sustainable energy: renewables (hydro, wind, solar and other) plus nuclear. That is the definition Cambridge uses: its 52.4% sustainable share is 42.6% renewables plus 9.8% nuclear (Cambridge Digital Mining Industry Report, April 2025, page 67).

  • Cambridge's figure is a snapshotCambridge's 59.4% comes from a single survey, in December 2025. Since then I move the grid-connected part of mining (91.9%) with the world grid's clean share, and hold off-grid mining as surveyed (Grid greening). The world grid's clean share rose by ~1.1 percentage points a year from 2019 to 2025 (36.5% to 42.9%), by 1.5 points in the 12 months to September 2026, and by 1.0 point since the Cambridge survey (~1.3 a year). At the long-run pace that adds ~1.4 percentage points a year to the mining share until Cambridge's next survey resets it. So far it has moved from 59.4% to 60.6%. New mining sites built on clean power won't show up until that next survey.

  • The points on the chartThe white diamonds are Cambridge's own estimates: 52.4%, from its survey of mining firms as of June 2024 (published April 2025), and 59.4% in December 2025. The orange dots are BEEST 1's estimates. The first marks February 2023, when I first reported that Bitcoin mining had passed 50% sustainable energy, at 52.6% (BEEST 1 write-up). The second is 58.8% for November 2026, from a model last tuned in October 2024.

Bitcoin Network Emissions

BEEST 2 (Bitcoin Energy & Emissions Sustainability Tracker) is my tracker of the Bitcoin network's emissions, updated daily since September 2019. It shows total emissions from the electricity the network uses, the methane miners destroy at oil-well flares, and the net of the two. Hashrate and BTC price sit on their own faint scales for context. I set out every input and its source in the full methodology; here is the short version.

  • How I calculate itEach day I multiply the network's hashrate (7-day average) by the fleet's efficiency to get electricity use (hashrate, network efficiency). I multiply that by the carbon intensity of the mining energy mix to get total emissions (carbon intensity), then subtract the methane destroyed at flare sites to get net emissions (emission mitigation).
  • Fleet efficiencyI estimate the network runs at ~19.3 J/TH at the facility meter in November 2026, within a range of 17.3 to 22.2 depending on the assumptions. I built that figure from Luxor's and Cambridge's 2026 fleet data (how the efficiency figure is built).
  • Energy mixI start from Cambridge's December 2025 survey, which puts the mining mix at 59.4% low-carbon and 252 gCO2e/kWh. Since then I move the grid-connected part with the world grid each month (grid greening).
  • MethaneI use Cambridge's figure for its 44 flare-gas respondents: 4.69 MtCO2e a year destroyed, on the 20-year warming potential of methane and the field-measured 91.1% flare destruction efficiency (emission mitigation).
  • Before December 2025I built the series back to September 2019 from BEEST 1 and Cambridge's survey points (history before December 2025). I first published BEEST 1's figures in February 2023 (BEEST 1 write-up), and the dynamic BEEST 1 charts I built with Willy Woo went public on 7 March 2023.
  • Why the chart slightly overestimates emissionsThree of my inputs come from surveys that are only refreshed every so often: how efficient the mining fleet is, how much mining runs on new low-carbon sites, and how much flare gas miners destroy. All three keep improving between surveys, and the chart only catches up when the next survey lands. Because of that lag, I expect the chart to show net emissions slightly higher than they really are, and by a little more the longer it has been since the last survey (the lag effect, limitations).
  • Sources
    • Hashrate, 7-day average: mempool.space
    • Fleet efficiency: Cambridge Digital Mining Industry Report, 2nd edition, preliminary results (20.8 J/TH, December 2025 survey) and Luxor Hashrate Lookback, July 2026; before December 2025, Cambridge's survey points with the CBECI hardware-efficiency series between them
    • Carbon intensity: BEEST 1 (September 2019 to October 2024) and Cambridge Digital Mining Industry Report, 2nd edition, preliminary results (252 gCO2e/kWh, December 2025)
    • Grid greening from December 2025: Ember monthly electricity data, with the on-grid share from Cambridge Digital Mining Industry Report, April 2025
    • Flare-gas mitigation: Cambridge Digital Mining Industry Report, 2nd edition, preliminary results (4.69 MtCO2e a year from 44 respondents) and, before December 2025, BEEST 1
    • Flare efficiency, 91.1%: Plant et al. 2022, Science; warming potential of methane over 20 years: IPCC AR6, Chapter 7
    • BTC price: Coin Metrics
